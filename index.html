Welcome

A community built on service

Shram Sanskriti Society UK (SSS UK) brings together people of Nepalese heritage, and friends of Nepal, who believe that communities are strongest when everyone contributes.

Our name comes from Shram Sanskriti — a culture that honours labour, voluntary service and ethical living. Inspired by the traditions of collective work and mutual support that have sustained village life in Nepal for generations, we create opportunities for people across the UK to volunteer, celebrate their heritage, take part in civic life and support one another.

We are an independent, voluntary and non-profit association. Every penny we raise goes towards our aims, and every role in the Society is grounded in service to the community.

Our areas of work

Three colours, one purpose

The three bars in our logo represent the three areas where we focus our work.

Environment & Nature

Deep Green

We believe the land, water, forests and every living thing deserve our care. We support environmental awareness and community-led projects that protect nature, both here in the United Kingdom and in Nepal.

Science & Technology

Deep Blue

Knowledge grows when it is shared. We encourage education, skills development and the exchange of expertise between our communities in the UK and Nepal, so that learning benefits everyone.

Arts, Culture & Heritage

Deep Yellow

Nepal’s many languages, traditions and art forms are a living inheritance. We celebrate them, preserve them and pass them on, especially to young people growing up in Britain.

Our journey

How the Society began

  1. 16 May 2026

    The Standing Volunteer Committee is formed

    A group of community members came together as the Standing Volunteer Committee to lay the foundations of the Society. Manoj Pun chaired the Committee and Dr Maha Rai led the drafting of the Society’s Constitution.

  2. 16 August 2026

    Members shape the Constitution

    At the first General Meeting, members reviewed the draft Constitution together, discussing how the Society should be run, how decisions should be made and what it should stand for.

  3. 1 November 2026

    The First General Convention

    Members formally adopted the Constitution and elected the Society’s first Executive Volunteer Committee, marking the official beginning of Shram Sanskriti Society UK.

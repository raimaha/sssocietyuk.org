About the Society

Service before self, community before all

Shram Sanskriti Society UK (SSS UK) brings together members of the Nepalese diaspora in the United Kingdom through voluntary service, civic responsibility, cultural preservation, community solidarity and the ethical principles of Shram Sanskriti — the culture of respect for labour.

We honour all the communities of Nepal, whose traditions of collective labour, mutual aid, respect for elders, harmony with nature and decision by common agreement have sustained village life for generations. These traditions guide the way we govern ourselves: by consensus, with every voice heard, and with leadership understood as a responsibility of service rather than an entitlement to authority.

Guiding Principles · Article 2.8

Rooted in the shared traditions of Nepal

In all our work and decisions we are guided by seven principles drawn from the community life of Nepal.

1

Dignity of labour (Shram)

All work, manual and intellectual, is honoured equally, and every member contributes according to their ability.

2

Mutual aid (Parma)

In the tradition of reciprocal labour, members support one another and the community — help given is help shared.

3

Decision by agreement

Decisions are reached together through patient dialogue in which every voice is heard, as in the community assemblies of Nepal.

4

Respect for elders and youth

The experience of elders is sought and valued, and the voices of young people are given equal space.

5

Harmony with nature

The land, water, forests and all living things are respected and cared for.

6

Equality of communities

All ethnic, linguistic, cultural and regional communities of Nepal, and all genders, are of equal worth and fairly represented.

7

Leadership as service

Those who hold office serve the community and are accountable to it.

Our objectives

  • Promote voluntary service, respect for labour, collective responsibility and active community participation.
  • Encourage ethical leadership, transparency, accountability, good governance and informed civic engagement.
  • Preserve, promote and celebrate the diverse cultural, linguistic, ethnic and historical heritage of Nepal.
  • Strengthen unity, mutual respect and cooperation among Nepalese communities and with the wider society in the UK.
  • Support educational, environmental, cultural, social and community-development initiatives.
  • Encourage participation by women, young people, older people and under-represented members of the community.
  • Facilitate knowledge exchange, skills development and cooperation between communities in the UK and Nepal.
  • Support community-led initiatives in environmental protection, public awareness, heritage, health awareness and sustainable development.
  • Build constructive relationships with organisations and institutions that share compatible objectives.
  • Promote peaceful dialogue, non-discrimination, social inclusion and respect for human dignity.
  • Pass on the knowledge, languages, oral traditions and customs of all Nepal’s communities to younger generations raised in the UK.

 Preamble

We, the founding members of Shram Sanskriti Society UK, united by our commitment to voluntary service, civic responsibility, cultural heritage, equality, integrity, environmental stewardship and community solidarity, establish this Constitution.

Recognising the diversity of the Nepalese diaspora in the United Kingdom, and the value of cooperation between communities in the United Kingdom and Nepal, we seek to promote a society founded on dignity, mutual respect, accountability and collective responsibility.

We honour all the communities of Nepal, whose traditions of collective labour, mutual aid, respect for elders, harmony with nature and decision by common agreement have sustained village and community life for generations. These traditions guide the way this Society governs itself.

Inspired by the philosophy and values of Shram Sanskriti, we affirm that voluntary service, respect for labour, ethical leadership, cultural preservation and community participation are fundamental to sustainable social progress.

We further affirm our commitment to democratic participation, to decision-making by consensus in which every voice is heard before any decision is made, and to transparency and lawful conduct, and to the principle that leadership is a responsibility of service rather than an entitlement to authority.

Accordingly, we adopt this Constitution as the supreme governing document of Shram Sanskriti Society UK.

Article 1Name, Identity and Interpretation

1.1Name

The name of the organisation is Shram Sanskriti Society UK, abbreviated as SSS UK (the “Society”).

1.2Official Identity

The Society may adopt an official logo, emblem and other identifying materials. The adoption or material alteration of the official logo requires approval by the Executive Committee and confirmation by the General Assembly.

The Society’s official colours, shown as three bars in its logo, represent the following areas of its work:

ColourRepresents
Deep Green (#075239)Environment and Nature
Deep Blue (#173775)Science and Technology
Deep Yellow (#D4A20B)Arts, Culture and Heritage

1.3Use of Name and Identity

The name, logo and other identifying materials of the Society may be used only for purposes authorised by the Society. No person may use the Society’s identity for personal, commercial, unlawful or otherwise unauthorised purposes.

1.4Definitions

In this Constitution:

  1. Society means Shram Sanskriti Society UK.
  2. General Assembly means the collective body of all Eligible Voting Members.
  3. General Convention means a formally convened meeting of the General Assembly for elections, constitutional matters, strategic decisions or other significant business.
  4. Annual General Meeting (AGM) means the annual meeting of the General Assembly held under Article 9.
  5. Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) means any meeting of the General Assembly other than the AGM or a General Convention.
  6. Executive Committee means the elected governing and coordinating committee established under Articles 6 and 7.
  7. Executive Committee Member means a person elected or co-opted to a voting position on the Executive Committee in accordance with this Constitution.
  8. General Volunteer Member means a member who undertakes or supports voluntary activities but does not, by that status alone, hold Executive Committee office.
  9. Eligible Voting Member means a member who satisfies Article 5.6.
  10. Consensus means a decision reached through the process in Article 9.8, in which every member present has had the opportunity to speak and no Substantive Objection remains. Consensus does not require that everyone agrees with every detail; a member may support a decision with reservations or Stand Aside.
  11. Ordinary Resolution means a resolution on routine governance or organisational business that does not require a higher threshold under this Constitution.
  12. Special Resolution means a resolution on a matter for which this Constitution requires the higher threshold in Article 15.4, namely constitutional amendments, changes of legal status and dissolution.
  13. Clear days means days excluding the day on which notice is given and the day of the meeting.
  14. Interim Coordinating Committee means the committee of founding members (also known as the Standing Committee) that has coordinated the establishment of the Society before the adoption of this Constitution.
  15. Standing Orders means rules, policies and procedures made under Article 6.7.
  16. Stand Aside means a member’s decision not to support a proposal while allowing the meeting to proceed with it; it does not prevent Consensus.
  17. Substantive Objection means a reasoned objection that a proposal conflicts with this Constitution, the Society’s objectives or Guiding Principles, or the law, or would cause serious harm to the Society or its members.

1.5Interpretation

The singular includes the plural where appropriate. A reference to an office includes a person lawfully acting in that office. Headings are for convenience only and do not affect interpretation.

1.6Precedence

If any policy, Standing Order or committee resolution is inconsistent with this Constitution, this Constitution prevails, subject always to applicable law.

Article 2Legal Status, Character and Principles

2.1Legal Status

The Society is an independent, unincorporated, voluntary and non-profit association established in the United Kingdom. It shall not describe itself as a registered charity, incorporated body or other regulated legal entity unless it has lawfully acquired that status.

2.2Principal Location

The Society is based in the United Kingdom. Its principal administrative address shall be decided and recorded by the Executive Committee.

2.3Non-Profit Character

The income and property of the Society shall be applied solely to its objectives. No part of its income or property shall be distributed to members by way of profit. This does not prevent reimbursement of reasonable, properly authorised expenses, or lawful payment for goods or services genuinely supplied to the Society, subject to the conflict-of-interest safeguards in Article 10.10.

2.4Independence

The Society controls its own constitution, governance, membership, finances, bank accounts, programmes, appointments and decisions. No external organisation or individual has governing authority over the Society unless that authority is established by an amendment to this Constitution.

2.5Relationship with the Shram Sanskriti Philosophy

The Society draws inspiration from the principles of Shram Sanskriti, including respect for labour, voluntary service, cultural identity, ethical leadership, social responsibility and community-led development. This alignment does not, by itself, create any legal or organisational affiliation with an external body.

2.6Political and Civic Activities

The Society may undertake lawful civic education, public discussion, advocacy and community engagement consistent with its objectives.

  1. The Society shall not operate as a political party in the United Kingdom.
  2. Any political activity, collaboration, donation or expenditure shall comply with applicable UK law, including electoral and political finance law.
  3. No Society funds or resources may be used to support an election campaign, candidate or political party unless the Executive Committee has first obtained appropriate legal advice and the General Assembly has approved the proposal.

2.7Compliance with Law

The Society and its members shall comply with applicable laws and regulatory obligations, including those relating to financial administration, fundraising, equality, safeguarding, data protection and public activities.

2.8Guiding Principles

The Society draws on the shared community traditions of Nepal. In all its work and decisions it shall be guided by the following principles:

  1. Dignity of labour (Shram) — all work, manual and intellectual, is honoured equally, and every member contributes according to their ability;
  2. Mutual aid (Parma) — in the tradition of reciprocal labour, members support one another and the community, and help given is help shared;
  3. Decision by agreement — decisions are reached together through patient dialogue in which every voice is heard, as in the community assemblies of Nepal;
  4. Respect for elders and youth — the experience of elders is sought and valued, and the voices of young people are given equal space;
  5. Harmony with nature — the land, water, forests and all living things are respected and cared for;
  6. Equality of communities — all ethnic, linguistic, cultural and regional communities of Nepal, and all genders, are of equal worth and are fairly represented; and
  7. Leadership as service — those who hold office serve the community and are accountable to it.
Article 3Aims and Objectives

3.1Principal Aim

The principal aim of the Society is to unite and engage members of the Nepalese diaspora in the United Kingdom through voluntary service, civic responsibility, cultural preservation, community solidarity and the ethical principles of Shram Sanskriti.

3.2Objectives

In furtherance of its principal aim, the Society shall seek to:

  1. promote voluntary service, respect for labour, collective responsibility and active community participation;
  2. encourage ethical leadership, transparency, accountability, good governance and informed civic engagement;
  3. preserve, promote and celebrate the diverse cultural, linguistic, ethnic and historical heritage of Nepal;
  4. strengthen unity, mutual respect and cooperation among Nepalese communities and with the wider society in the United Kingdom;
  5. support educational, environmental, cultural, social and community-development initiatives;
  6. encourage participation by women, young people, older people and under-represented members of the community;
  7. facilitate knowledge exchange, skills development and cooperation between communities in the United Kingdom and Nepal;
  8. support lawful community-led initiatives in environmental protection, public awareness, heritage preservation, health awareness and sustainable development;
  9. build constructive relationships with organisations and institutions that share compatible objectives;
  10. promote peaceful dialogue, non-discrimination, social inclusion and respect for human dignity; and
  11. respect, preserve and pass on the knowledge, languages, oral traditions and customary practices of all the communities of Nepal, particularly to younger generations born or raised in the United Kingdom.

3.3Application of Objectives

All programmes, partnerships and expenditure shall be consistent with the objectives in this Article.

Article 4Powers of the Society

4.1General Power

Subject to this Constitution and applicable law, the Society may do anything lawful that is reasonably necessary to achieve its objectives.

4.2Specific Powers

In particular, the Society may:

  1. organise meetings, conferences, seminars, workshops, cultural programmes and community events;
  2. undertake educational, environmental, social and voluntary-service projects;
  3. raise and receive funds through membership subscriptions, donations, grants, sponsorship, fundraising events and other lawful sources;
  4. open and operate bank accounts in the Society’s name, subject to the requirements of the bank;
  5. enter into agreements and partnerships;
  6. establish working groups, subcommittees and project teams;
  7. appoint advisers and volunteers;
  8. publish information and educational materials;
  9. acquire, use and maintain equipment and other property needed for its activities;
  10. arrange insurance; and
  11. do any other lawful thing incidental to its objectives.

4.3Authority to Enter Agreements

No person may enter into a contract or other legally binding commitment on behalf of the Society without prior authority from the Executive Committee. The Chair is the principal authorised signatory unless the Executive Committee formally authorises another person. Every such authorisation shall be minuted and shall state the nature and extent of the authority granted.

4.4Financial Commitments

All financial commitments are subject to the Society’s approved financial procedures and available resources.

Article 5Membership

5.1Eligibility

Membership is open to persons of Nepalese origin, members of the Nepalese diaspora and any other person with a genuine interest in supporting the Society’s aims, subject to the membership rules and applicable equality law. Every applicant shall agree to uphold this Constitution and the lawful objectives of the Society.

5.2Membership and Recognised Roles

There is one class of voting membership: Annual Membership, held by a person admitted to membership whose annual subscription is paid and current. In addition, the Society recognises the following roles, which do not create separate classes of membership or voting rights:

  1. Founding Member — an honorary recognition under Article 18 for a person who contributed to establishing the Society;
  2. General Volunteer Member — a member who supports the Society through voluntary service, programmes, activities or community engagement.

A person may hold more than one of these roles at the same time, and may also be an Executive Committee Member.

5.3Subscription

The annual membership subscription is initially £10. Any change requires approval by the General Assembly on the recommendation of the Executive Committee. The Executive Committee may set a reduced or waived rate for hardship, applied fairly and recorded in the Standing Orders.

5.4Admission, Renewal and Register

The Executive Committee shall set procedures for applications, admission and renewal. An accurate and secure register of members shall be kept. A membership that is not renewed within 60 days of its renewal date shall lapse, and the person may rejoin by fresh application.

5.5Rights of Members

Every Eligible Voting Member has the right to:

  1. attend the AGM, any EGM and any General Convention;
  2. take part in discussions and consultations;
  3. submit proposals in accordance with the Standing Orders;
  4. vote on matters before the General Assembly;
  5. stand for election, subject to the eligibility requirements of the office; and
  6. receive appropriate information about the Society’s governance, activities and finances.

5.6Eligible Voting Members

A member is an Eligible Voting Member if they are aged 18 or over, their membership is current, and they are not suspended under Article 5.11.

5.7Equality of Voting Rights

Each Eligible Voting Member has one vote at meetings of the General Assembly. No member receives additional votes because they are a Founding Member, Executive Committee Member, adviser or holder of any other position. Proxy voting is not permitted unless the General Assembly approves rules for it in the Standing Orders.

5.8Responsibilities of Members

Members shall:

  1. uphold this Constitution and the Society’s lawful objectives;
  2. act honestly, respectfully and responsibly;
  3. respect the rights and dignity of other members;
  4. comply with the Society’s policies;
  5. avoid conduct that materially harms the Society or its members; and
  6. declare relevant conflicts of interest.

5.9External Organisations

Membership of the Society does not confer membership of any political party, movement, association or other external organisation.

5.10Resignation

A member may resign by written notice (including email) to the General Secretary.

  1. The resignation shall be acknowledged by the Chair and recorded by the Executive Committee at its next meeting.
  2. The resignation takes effect when acknowledged by the Chair, or 14 days after the notice is received, whichever is earlier.
  3. An Executive Committee Member who resigns from office shall, before the resignation takes effect where reasonably possible, hand over all records, property and responsibilities of the office to the Chair or a person the Executive Committee nominates.
  4. Subscriptions already paid are not refundable.

5.11Suspension and Termination

Membership may be suspended or terminated for serious misconduct, material breach of this Constitution or other substantial grounds, following a fair procedure under Article 12.

  1. Except where immediate protective action is reasonably necessary, the member shall receive written notice of the allegations and a reasonable opportunity to respond.
  2. Any suspension pending investigation shall be proportionate, limited in time and subject to review.
  3. The member may appeal a final decision under Article 12.6.

5.12Membership Records

Membership records shall be kept securely and processed in accordance with data protection law.

Article 6Governance Structure

6.1Governing Bodies

The Society operates through:

  1. the General Assembly;
  2. the Executive Committee; and
  3. such advisory bodies, regional structures and working groups as are established under this Constitution.

6.2General Assembly

The General Assembly is the supreme decision-making body of the Society and comprises all Eligible Voting Members.

6.3Powers Reserved to the General Assembly

The General Assembly has authority to:

  1. elect the Executive Committee;
  2. receive and consider annual reports;
  3. approve the annual financial statements;
  4. set major organisational priorities;
  5. approve changes to membership subscriptions;
  6. confirm the appointment of advisers and the Society’s logo;
  7. approve any formal affiliation with an external organisation;
  8. amend this Constitution;
  9. approve changes to the Society’s legal status;
  10. resolve to dissolve the Society; and
  11. decide any other matter reserved to members by this Constitution.

6.4Executive Committee

The Executive Committee is responsible for the governance, administration, coordination and delivery of the Society’s activities between meetings of the General Assembly, and is accountable to the General Assembly.

6.5Collective Responsibility

The Executive Committee works through collective leadership, shared responsibility and decision by Consensus under Article 9.8. No officer has greater voting power because of a senior position, and no officer has a casting vote.

6.6Delegation

The Executive Committee may delegate specified responsibilities to officers, subcommittees or volunteers. Delegation does not remove the Executive Committee’s responsibility for proper oversight. Every delegation shall be minuted.

6.7Standing Orders and Policies

The Executive Committee may make Standing Orders and policies on meeting procedure, membership administration, finance, safeguarding, data protection, conduct and other matters needed to implement this Constitution. Standing Orders must be consistent with this Constitution, shall be made available to members, and may be amended or revoked by the General Assembly by Ordinary Resolution.

Article 7Executive Committee: Composition and Responsibilities

7.1Composition

The Executive Committee comprises the following elected positions:

  1. Chair — Lead Coordinator and Chief Volunteer;
  2. Vice Chair Coordinator — Membership and Records;
  3. Vice Chair Coordinator — Programmes and Community Engagement;
  4. Vice Chair Coordinator — Fundraising and Resource Development;
  5. Vice Chair Coordinator — Partnerships and External Relations;
  6. Vice Chair Coordinator — Equality, Diversity and Inclusion;
  7. General Secretary — Chief Administrative and Governance Coordinator;
  8. Treasurer — Finance and Accountability Coordinator; and
  9. such number of ordinary Executive Committee Members and voting Regional Volunteer Leads as the General Assembly decides before each election.

All voting positions are filled by election under Article 8, or by co-option to a vacancy under Article 8.9.

7.2Eligibility

Every Executive Committee Member must be an Eligible Voting Member throughout their term of office. No person may hold more than one of the offices listed in Article 7.1(a) to (h) at the same time, and the Chair and Treasurer shall not be members of the same household.

7.3Chair

The Chair shall:

  1. provide overall leadership and coordination;
  2. chair Executive Committee meetings;
  3. promote consensus and collective responsibility;
  4. represent the Society in authorised external engagements;
  5. support other officers in their roles;
  6. ensure that matters requiring collective approval go to the appropriate body; and
  7. act as an authorised signatory.

The Chair has no unilateral authority over matters reserved for collective decision.

7.4Vice Chair Coordinator — Membership and Records

The Vice Chair Coordinator — Membership and Records shall:

  1. oversee membership registration and renewal;
  2. maintain the membership register;
  3. support membership engagement and participation;
  4. coordinate membership administration with the General Secretary and Treasurer; and
  5. ensure membership information is properly protected.

7.5Vice Chair Coordinator — Programmes and Community Engagement

The Vice Chair Coordinator — Programmes and Community Engagement shall:

  1. coordinate the Society’s programmes and events;
  2. develop annual activity plans;
  3. promote community participation and outreach; and
  4. support the safe and effective delivery of activities.

7.6Vice Chair Coordinator — Fundraising and Resource Development

The Vice Chair Coordinator — Fundraising and Resource Development shall:

  1. develop fundraising proposals and strategies;
  2. identify lawful funding opportunities;
  3. coordinate donor and sponsor engagement;
  4. work with the Treasurer to ensure financial accountability; and
  5. support the Society’s long-term sustainability.

7.7Vice Chair Coordinator — Partnerships and External Relations

The Vice Chair Coordinator — Partnerships and External Relations shall:

  1. develop relationships with relevant organisations and institutions;
  2. coordinate partnership opportunities in the United Kingdom, Nepal and internationally;
  3. support knowledge exchange and collaborative initiatives;
  4. represent the Society externally when authorised; and
  5. refer proposed partnerships for the required approval.

7.8Vice Chair Coordinator — Equality, Diversity and Inclusion

The Vice Chair Coordinator — Equality, Diversity and Inclusion shall:

  1. promote equality and inclusive participation;
  2. support fair access to leadership and volunteering opportunities;
  3. advise on inclusive organisational practice;
  4. encourage representation of under-represented groups; and
  5. support the aim in Article 7.15.

7.9General Secretary

The General Secretary is the principal administrative and governance coordinator and shall:

  1. coordinate official correspondence and administrative procedures;
  2. prepare meeting notices, agendas and supporting papers;
  3. keep minutes, resolutions and official records;
  4. circulate minutes within 14 days of a meeting wherever reasonably practicable;
  5. keep the official Constitution, Standing Orders and approved policies;
  6. monitor implementation of decisions;
  7. coordinate administrative communication between officers and members;
  8. support preparation of the AGM and any General Convention; and
  9. prepare the annual administrative report.

The General Secretary may coordinate the review of governance documents but may not amend this Constitution except as approved under Article 15.

7.10Treasurer

The Treasurer shall:

  1. keep accurate financial records;
  2. oversee receipts, payments and banking;
  3. record membership subscriptions, donations, grants and expenditure;
  4. present financial reports to the Executive Committee;
  5. prepare the annual financial statements;
  6. support financial planning and budgeting;
  7. ensure payments are properly authorised; and
  8. coordinate the independent financial review and insurance arrangements.

7.11Regional Volunteer Leads

Regional Volunteer Leads may be established on a county or other agreed geographical basis within the United Kingdom to coordinate local participation, activities and communication. The Executive Committee decides regional boundaries and responsibilities. A Regional Volunteer Lead has Executive Committee voting rights only if elected to a voting position under Article 7.1(i).

7.12Executive Committee Members

Executive Committee Members shall attend meetings, contribute constructively, take part in governance and decision-making, and carry out responsibilities allocated by collective agreement.

7.13General Volunteer Members

General Volunteer Members may assist with programmes, fundraising, events, communications and other activities. They have full rights at General Assembly meetings if they are Eligible Voting Members, but have no Executive Committee voting rights by that status.

7.14Assistant Officers

The Executive Committee may appoint Assistant Secretaries, Assistant Treasurers or other assistant officers from among its own Executive Committee Members where needed. An Executive Committee Member appointed as an assistant officer keeps their full Executive Committee voting rights, but the appointment does not give them any additional vote.

7.15Gender Representation

The Society aims for at least 50% female representation on the Executive Committee. It shall pursue this through fair nomination procedures, encouragement and inclusive leadership development, in accordance with applicable law.

Article 8Elections, Terms of Office and Vacancies

8.1Term of Office

The Executive Committee serves a term of three years, beginning when the election results are declared. Executive Committee Members may stand for re-election.

8.2Timing of Elections

Executive Committee elections take place at a General Convention or at a General Assembly meeting convened for that purpose.

8.3Election Committee

An independent Election Committee of three to five Eligible Voting Members administers each election. It is appointed by the Executive Committee, and its membership shall be notified to members. No member of the Election Committee may stand as a candidate in the election it administers.

8.4Appointment Date

The Election Committee shall be appointed at least 30 days before the scheduled election, except for the first election under Article 17.4.

8.5Nominations

Each candidate must be nominated by two Eligible Voting Members, must confirm in writing their willingness to serve, and must meet the eligibility requirements of the office. The Election Committee shall publish the nomination deadline in the notice of the meeting.

8.6Uncontested Positions

Where only one eligible candidate is nominated for a position, the Election Committee may present that candidate for confirmation by Consensus. If Consensus is not reached, the candidate requires the support of at least 65% of members present and eligible to vote.

8.7Contested Positions

Where more than one eligible candidate is nominated:

  1. the election shall be by secret ballot, unless the General Assembly has approved another fair and transparent method;
  2. a candidate who receives at least 65% of the valid votes cast is elected;
  3. if no candidate receives 65%, a second ballot is held between the two candidates with the most votes, and a candidate who receives at least 65% of the valid votes cast in that ballot is elected;
  4. if neither candidate receives 65% in the second ballot, the Election Committee shall allow a short period for dialogue under Article 9.8, after which a final ballot is held between the same two candidates and the candidate with more valid votes is elected; and
  5. a tied final ballot shall be repeated once; if the tie remains, the Election Committee shall refer the matter to the General Assembly to resolve by an agreed method, which may include drawing lots.

8.8Declaration of Results

The Election Committee shall verify and formally announce the results. The General Secretary shall keep the election records for at least three years.

8.9Vacancies

If a position becomes vacant during a term, the Executive Committee may co-opt an eligible member to fill it until the next General Assembly meeting. At that meeting an election shall be held for the remainder of the term.

8.10Removal from Office

An Executive Committee Member may be removed for serious misconduct, material breach of duty or persistent failure to fulfil their responsibilities.

  1. Absence from three consecutive Executive Committee meetings without reasonable explanation may be grounds for review.
  2. Before removal, the member shall receive written notice of the concerns and a reasonable opportunity to respond.
  3. Removal requires a resolution supported by at least 65% of the other Executive Committee Members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting.
  4. The member may appeal to the General Assembly, whose decision is final.

8.11Continuity

If elections cannot reasonably be completed before a term expires, the existing Executive Committee continues in a caretaker capacity until elections are held. A caretaker Committee shall not take decisions outside the ordinary course of business. Any extension beyond six months requires General Assembly approval.

8.12End of Election Committee Duties

The Election Committee’s duties end once the results are formally recorded and the newly elected Executive Committee has taken office.

8.13Early Elections

The General Assembly need not wait for the end of a three-year term to elect a new Executive Committee.

  1. The General Assembly may resolve, at an AGM or EGM, to hold early elections for the whole Executive Committee. The proposal shall be considered through the consensus process in Article 9.8; where Consensus is not reached, it requires the votes in favour of at least 65% of members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting.
  2. Early elections shall also be held if more than half of the Executive Committee positions are vacant at the same time.
  3. Early elections shall take place within 60 days of the resolution or vacancies, in accordance with this Article. The outgoing Executive Committee continues in a caretaker capacity under Article 8.11 until then.
  4. The newly elected Executive Committee serves a full three-year term.
Article 9Meetings, Quorum and Decision-Making

9.1Types of Meeting

The Society may hold Executive Committee meetings, AGMs, EGMs, General Conventions, and advisory and subcommittee meetings.

9.2Executive Committee Meetings

The Executive Committee shall meet at least three times a year and otherwise as required. Any five Executive Committee Members may, by written request, ask the Chair to call a meeting, which shall then be held within 21 days of the request.

9.3Executive Committee Quorum

The quorum is more than half of the current voting Executive Committee Members. An inquorate meeting may discuss matters but may not make binding decisions.

9.4Holding the AGM

An AGM shall be held once in each calendar year, with no more than 15 months between one AGM and the next.

9.5Calling an EGM

The Executive Committee may call an EGM at any time. It shall call an EGM within 28 days of receiving a written request, stating the business, from at least 10% of Eligible Voting Members or 20 Eligible Voting Members, whichever is fewer. If it fails to do so, the members making the request may call the meeting themselves.

9.6General Assembly Quorum

The quorum for an AGM, EGM or General Convention is 20% of Eligible Voting Members or 20 Eligible Voting Members, whichever is fewer, but never fewer than five. If there are fewer than five Eligible Voting Members, all must be present.

9.7Inquorate General Assembly Meeting

If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes of the start time, the meeting shall be adjourned to a date at least 7 and no more than 28 days later, of which members shall be notified. Business requiring a Special Resolution may not be decided at an adjourned meeting unless it is quorate.

9.8Consensus Process

Consensus is the Society’s way of making decisions. Voting is a last resort, used only when the process below has been followed and agreement cannot be reached. The person chairing a meeting acts as facilitator, not as decision-maker.

  1. Preparation. Wherever possible, a proposal shall be circulated with the agenda, so that members can reflect on it and consult others before the meeting.
  2. Every voice heard. Before any decision, the facilitator shall invite each member present to speak in turn, without interruption. No member is required to speak, but no decision is taken until everyone has had the opportunity. Particular care shall be taken to hear elders, young people, women and anyone who has not yet spoken.
  3. Refining the proposal. The meeting shall seek to improve the proposal so that it meets the concerns raised.
  4. Testing for consensus. The facilitator shall restate the proposal and ask each member whether they agree, agree with reservations, Stand Aside, or have a Substantive Objection.
  5. Standing aside. A member who Stands Aside does not prevent the decision. Reservations and stand-asides shall be recorded in the minutes. If three or more members Stand Aside on the same proposal, the facilitator shall ask whether the meeting wishes to continue discussion before deciding.
  6. Substantive Objection. A member raising a Substantive Objection shall give reasons and, where possible, suggest an alternative.
  7. Time for dialogue. If a Substantive Objection remains, the matter shall be deferred. The facilitator may ask a small group, including the objecting members and, where helpful, a respected elder or adviser, to seek a solution and report back at the next meeting or within 30 days.
  8. Last resort. If Consensus is still not reached when the matter returns, it may be decided by vote under Article 9.9, or under Article 15 or 16 where those apply.
  9. Urgent matters. Where delay would cause serious harm to the Society or its members, the meeting may proceed to a vote after the first round in paragraph (b). The reason for urgency shall be recorded in the minutes.

9.9Ordinary Resolutions

Where Consensus is not reached after the process in Article 9.8, an Ordinary Resolution requires the votes in favour of at least 65% of members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting.

9.10Counting Votes

Any fractional voting requirement is rounded up to the next whole number. Abstentions do not count as votes in favour but are included in the number of members present and eligible to vote. A proposal that does not reach the required threshold is not carried.

9.11Special Resolutions

Constitutional amendments, changes of legal status and dissolution require the approval set out in Articles 15 and 16.

9.12Notice Periods

The following minimum notice shall be given to all Eligible Voting Members:

9.13Giving Notice

Notice may be given by email, post or any other method recorded in the Standing Orders, to the contact details in the membership register. Notice shall state the date, time, place or remote-access arrangements, and the business. Accidental failure to give notice to a member does not invalidate the meeting.

9.14AGM Business

The AGM shall ordinarily:

  1. receive the Chair’s annual report;
  2. receive the General Secretary’s administrative report;
  3. approve the Treasurer’s annual financial statements;
  4. receive the independent financial review report;
  5. review activities and priorities;
  6. consider proposals submitted under the Standing Orders; and
  7. hold elections where due.

9.15Participation

All Eligible Voting Members, including General Volunteer Members, may take part and vote at General Assembly meetings.

9.16Observers at Executive Committee Meetings

Members who are not Executive Committee Members may attend by invitation or with the Committee’s approval. They may contribute to discussion but may not vote.

9.17Remote Participation

Meetings may be held in person, remotely or in hybrid form, provided participants can communicate effectively and voting eligibility can be verified. A member taking part remotely counts towards the quorum.

9.18Minutes

Minutes shall record attendance, apologies, declarations of interest, significant decisions, whether each was reached by Consensus or by vote, any reservations, stand-asides and Substantive Objections, voting outcomes and assigned responsibilities. The General Secretary keeps the official record.

Article 10Finance, Property and Financial Accountability

10.1Use of Funds

All funds and property shall be used only for the Society’s objectives.

10.2Bank Account

The Society shall keep a bank account in its official name, subject to the bank’s requirements. All money received on behalf of the Society shall be paid into that account without undue delay.

10.3Authorised Signatories

Bank signatories are approved by the Executive Committee and shall ordinarily include the Chair, the Treasurer and at least one other officer.

10.4Authorising Payments

Payments require authorisation under a Financial Management Policy approved by the Executive Committee.

  1. Where the bank allows, every payment requires approval by two authorised signatories.
  2. No person may authorise a payment to themselves or to a connected person.
  3. Expenses are reimbursed only against receipts or other reasonable evidence.

10.5Financial Records

The Treasurer shall keep accurate records of all income, expenditure, assets and liabilities, and shall keep them for at least six years.

10.6Financial Reporting

The Treasurer shall report to the Executive Committee at each ordinary meeting and prepare annual financial statements for approval by the AGM.

10.7Financial Year

The financial year is set by the Executive Committee and recorded in the Financial Management Policy.

10.8Independent Financial Review

The annual financial statements shall be independently reviewed before they are presented to the AGM. The reviewer shall be independent of the Society’s day-to-day finances and shall not be an Executive Committee Member. A formal audit shall be carried out where required by law, a funding agreement or a resolution of the General Assembly.

10.9Expenditure Outside the United Kingdom

Any funds transferred or committed to activities in Nepal or elsewhere shall:

  1. support the Society’s objectives;
  2. be properly authorised;
  3. be documented;
  4. be subject to reasonable checks on the recipient and the intended use; and
  5. comply with applicable financial, sanctions and other legal requirements.

10.10Conflicts of Interest

Any person with a financial or personal interest in a proposed decision or transaction shall declare it before the matter is discussed. That person shall not take part in the decision unless permitted under an approved conflict-of-interest policy and applicable law. Declarations shall be minuted.

10.11Insurance

The Executive Committee shall assess the Society’s activities and arrange appropriate insurance, including public liability insurance where events are held.

10.12Indemnity

Subject to applicable law and available funds, the Society may indemnify an officer or authorised volunteer against reasonable liabilities properly incurred in the lawful, good-faith performance of authorised duties. No indemnity extends to fraud, dishonesty, wilful misconduct or liabilities that cannot lawfully be indemnified. This provision does not create limited liability or remove any personal liability imposed by law.

Article 11Advisory Committee and Supporting Structures

11.1Establishment

The Executive Committee may establish an Advisory Committee to provide independent advice, professional guidance and institutional support.

11.2Composition

Advisers may be drawn from people with relevant experience in community service, culture, education, governance, finance, law, environmental protection or other relevant fields.

11.3Appointment

Advisers are nominated by the elected Executive Committee and confirmed by the members at a General Assembly meeting or General Convention.

11.4Term

Advisers serve for three years and may be reappointed.

11.5Status

Advisory Committee membership is honorary and consultative. Advisers have no Executive Committee voting rights by virtue of that role. Advisers who are Eligible Voting Members keep their ordinary General Assembly voting rights.

11.6Role

The Advisory Committee may advise on strategy, governance, partnerships, organisational development and other matters referred to it.

11.7Subcommittees and Working Groups

The Executive Committee may establish subcommittees or working groups for defined purposes. Their terms of reference, responsibilities and reporting arrangements shall be recorded in writing, and they shall report to the Executive Committee.

11.8Counsel of Elders

The Advisory Committee shall, where possible, include respected elders from the community, reflecting the diversity of Nepal’s communities. Elders may be invited to offer guidance on matters of culture and tradition, to support the consensus process under Article 9.8, and to help resolve disagreements under Article 12.2.

Article 12Conduct, Complaints and Dispute Resolution

12.1Standards of Conduct

Members and officers shall act with honesty, integrity, respect, fairness and accountability.

12.2Informal Resolution

Disagreements should first be addressed through respectful dialogue, with the aim of restoring good relations rather than assigning blame. Where helpful, the parties may agree to ask a respected elder, adviser or other impartial member to facilitate a conversation between them.

12.3Formal Complaints

A formal complaint may be made in writing to the General Secretary or, where the complaint concerns the General Secretary, to the Chair.

12.4Investigation Panel

Where a complaint cannot reasonably be resolved informally, the Executive Committee shall appoint an impartial panel of at least three people with no material involvement in the matter.

12.5Fair Procedure

The panel shall:

  1. consider the relevant evidence;
  2. give the people affected a reasonable opportunity to respond, and allow them to be accompanied by a person of their choice;
  3. maintain appropriate confidentiality; and
  4. set out its findings and recommendations in writing.

12.6Decisions and Appeals

Disciplinary decisions are made by the Executive Committee on the panel’s recommendation, excluding any member involved in the matter. A member affected by a final disciplinary decision may appeal in writing within 21 days to an appeal panel of three people appointed from members of the Advisory Committee or other impartial persons. No one who took part in the original decision may sit on the appeal panel. The appeal panel’s decision is final within the Society.

12.7Serious Matters

Nothing in this Article prevents anyone from reporting a matter to the police, a regulator or another competent authority, or from exercising their legal rights.

Article 13Equality, Diversity, Inclusion and Safeguarding

13.1Equality

The Society shall promote equality, dignity and respect in accordance with UK equality law.

13.2Non-Discrimination

The Society shall not unlawfully discriminate against, harass or victimise any person on any ground protected by law.

13.3Cultural Diversity

The Society respects and celebrates the diversity of Nepalese ethnic, linguistic, cultural and regional identities.

13.4Inclusive Participation

The Society shall seek to remove unreasonable barriers to participation and encourage fair access to membership, volunteering and leadership.

13.5Safeguarding

The Society shall adopt a safeguarding policy and appoint a designated safeguarding lead before running any activity involving children or adults at risk. Volunteers in such activities shall have appropriate checks where required by law.

13.6Data Protection

Personal information shall be collected, used, kept and disclosed only in accordance with data protection law and the Society’s approved procedures.

Article 14External Relationships and International Cooperation

14.1General Principle

The Society may cooperate with organisations, institutions and community initiatives in the United Kingdom, Nepal and internationally where this advances its objectives.

14.2Shram Sanskriti Movement in Nepal

The Society recognises the values and community-service philosophy of the Shram Sanskriti movement in Nepal and may establish lawful relationships with organisations and representatives associated with it.

14.3Overseas (Pravas) Structures

The Society may engage with an overseas (Pravas) structure established by an organisation in Nepal, after checking that structure’s governing rules and the requirements of applicable law. Any such relationship shall be set out in a written Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) approved by the Executive Committee, stating its purpose, the responsibilities of each party, any financial arrangements and how either party may end it. An MOU that creates a formal affiliation also requires General Assembly approval, and no MOU may transfer control over the Society contrary to Article 14.4.

14.4Independence of Governance

No external affiliation transfers control of the Society’s membership, finances, assets, elections or internal governance.

14.5Written Agreements

Significant collaborations shall be documented in written agreements setting out their purpose, responsibilities, financial arrangements and accountability.

14.6International Financial Transactions

International donations, grants, expenditure and transfers of resources shall comply with Article 10 and applicable law.

14.7External Representation

Only persons authorised by the Executive Committee may make commitments or statements on behalf of the Society.

Article 15Constitutional Amendments and Change of Legal Status

15.1Authority to Amend

This Constitution may be amended only by the General Assembly.

15.2Proposals

An amendment may be proposed by the Executive Committee, or by at least ten Eligible Voting Members in writing to the General Secretary at least 35 clear days before the meeting.

15.3Notice

The full text of a proposed amendment shall be sent to members at least 21 clear days before the meeting at which it will be considered.

15.4Approval

Amendments shall first be considered through the consensus process in Article 9.8. Where Consensus is not reached, an amendment requires the votes in favour of at least 75% of members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting (a Special Resolution).

15.5Change of Legal Status

A proposal to incorporate the Society, register it as a charity or under another legal framework, or otherwise materially change its legal status requires a Special Resolution under this Article.

15.6Effective Date

An amendment takes effect on the date stated in the resolution or, if none is stated, immediately on approval.

15.7Constitutional Record

The General Secretary shall keep a record of all amendments and the current approved version of this Constitution, and shall make it available to members.

Article 16Dissolution

16.1Authority

The Society may be dissolved only by resolution of the General Assembly at a meeting called specifically for that purpose.

16.2Notice

At least 30 clear days’ notice shall be given of a proposed dissolution.

16.3Approval

The proposal shall first be considered through the consensus process in Article 9.8. Where Consensus is not reached, dissolution requires the votes in favour of at least 75% of members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting.

16.4Settling Liabilities

Once dissolution is approved, the Executive Committee shall bring activities to an orderly close and settle all lawful debts and liabilities.

16.5Remaining Assets

Any remaining assets shall be transferred to one or more non-profit organisations with compatible civic, cultural, educational, environmental or community-benefit objectives in the United Kingdom or Nepal, as decided by the General Assembly, subject to applicable law and any restrictions attached to the assets.

16.6No Distribution to Members

No remaining assets shall be distributed among members.

16.7Final Records

The General Secretary and Treasurer shall arrange for the Society’s official and financial records to be kept or lawfully disposed of.

Article 17Adoption, Commencement and Transitional Provisions

17.1Adoption

This Constitution shall be presented to the First General Convention for adoption. It is adopted by Consensus of those present who have joined the Society or, failing Consensus, by the votes in favour of at least 75% of them.

17.2Commencement

This Constitution takes effect on the date recorded in the adopting resolution.

17.3Interim Arrangements

Until the first Executive Committee takes office, the Interim Coordinating Committee may continue to carry out necessary administrative and organisational functions within the authority previously granted to it.

17.4First Executive Committee

The first Executive Committee shall be elected or confirmed under transitional arrangements approved by the First General Convention. The notice periods in Article 9.12 and the timing in Article 8.4 do not apply to that first election. The first term ends at the AGM held in 2029.

17.5Transfer of Records

The Interim Coordinating Committee shall hand over all records, financial information, membership records and responsibilities to the first Executive Committee within 30 days of it taking office.

17.6Supersession

On commencement, this Constitution supersedes all earlier draft constitutional provisions, except any transitional decisions expressly preserved by the General Assembly.

Article 18Founding Members and Foundational Record

18.1Establishment

Shram Sanskriti Society UK was initiated by members of the Nepalese diaspora in the United Kingdom who shared a commitment to voluntary service, cultural preservation, community solidarity, ethical leadership and the principles of Shram Sanskriti.

18.2Recognition

Individuals who made a recognised contribution to the initiation, preparation and establishment of the Society before its formal inauguration may be recorded as Founding Members.

18.3Founding Register

The names of Founding Members shall be recorded in an official Founding Register, set out in Schedule 1, adopted with this Constitution and finalised by the first elected Executive Committee as provided in that Schedule.

18.4Status

Founding Member recognition is honorary and historical. It does not confer permanent Executive Committee membership, additional voting rights, preferential electoral status or exemption from ordinary membership requirements, including the annual subscription.

18.5Foundational Contributions

Voluntary financial and non-financial contributions made towards establishing the Society may be recorded in the Founding Register or in the financial records.

18.6Financial Accountability

All such financial contributions shall be properly recorded and kept separate from membership subscriptions and restricted project funds.

18.7Preservation of Records

The Founding Register and establishment records shall be kept as part of the Society’s official historical archive.

Governance

Governance structure

The Society operates through the General Assembly, the Executive Committee, and such advisory bodies, regional structures and working groups as are established.

Governing bodies Article 6.1

How the Society is organised

General AssemblyThe supreme decision-making body of the Society, comprising all Eligible Voting Members Article 6.2
Executive CommitteeResponsible for governance, administration, coordination and delivery between meetings of the General Assembly, and accountable to it Article 6.4
Advisory CommitteeIndependent advice, professional guidance and institutional support Article 11.1
Regional Volunteer LeadsLocal participation, activities and communication Article 7.11
Working groupsSubcommittees and working groups for defined purposes Article 11.7

Powers reserved to the General Assembly

Article 6.3 The General Assembly has authority to:

  • elect the Executive Committee;
  • receive and consider annual reports;
  • approve the annual financial statements;
  • set major organisational priorities;
  • approve changes to membership subscriptions;
  • confirm the appointment of advisers and the Society’s logo;
  • approve any formal affiliation with an external organisation;
  • amend the Constitution;
  • approve changes to the Society’s legal status; and
  • resolve to dissolve the Society.

Collective responsibility

Article 6.5 The Executive Committee works through collective leadership, shared responsibility and decision by Consensus. No officer has greater voting power because of a senior position, and no officer has a casting vote.

Article 6.6 The Executive Committee may delegate specified responsibilities to officers, subcommittees or volunteers. Delegation does not remove its responsibility for proper oversight.

Article 9.2 The Executive Committee shall meet at least three times a year and otherwise as required.

Consensus process Article 9.8

Consensus is the Society’s way of making decisions

Voting is a last resort, used only when the process below has been followed and agreement cannot be reached. The person chairing a meeting acts as facilitator, not as decision-maker.

  1. PreparationWherever possible, a proposal shall be circulated with the agenda, so that members can reflect on it and consult others before the meeting.
  2. Every voice heardThe facilitator shall invite each member present to speak in turn, without interruption. Particular care shall be taken to hear elders, young people, women and anyone who has not yet spoken.
  3. Refining the proposalThe meeting shall seek to improve the proposal so that it meets the concerns raised.
  4. Testing for consensusThe facilitator shall restate the proposal and ask each member whether they agree, agree with reservations, Stand Aside, or have a Substantive Objection.
  5. Standing asideA member who Stands Aside does not prevent the decision. Reservations and stand-asides shall be recorded in the minutes.
  6. Substantive ObjectionA member raising a Substantive Objection shall give reasons and, where possible, suggest an alternative.
  7. Time for dialogueIf a Substantive Objection remains, the matter shall be deferred, and a small group — including, where helpful, a respected elder or adviser — may seek a solution.
  8. Last resortIf Consensus is still not reached, an Ordinary Resolution requires the votes in favour of at least 65% of members present and eligible to vote at a quorate meeting Article 9.9.

Elections, terms of office and vacancies

Elections

Term of office Article 8.1

The Executive Committee serves a term of three years, beginning when the election results are declared. Executive Committee Members may stand for re-election.

Election Committee Article 8.3

An independent Election Committee of three to five Eligible Voting Members administers each election. No member of the Election Committee may stand as a candidate in the election it administers.

Nominations Article 8.5

Each candidate must be nominated by two Eligible Voting Members, must confirm in writing their willingness to serve, and must meet the eligibility requirements of the office.

Uncontested positions Article 8.6

Where only one eligible candidate is nominated, that candidate may be confirmed by Consensus. If Consensus is not reached, the candidate requires the support of at least 65% of members present and eligible to vote.

Contested positions Article 8.7

The election shall be by secret ballot. A candidate who receives at least 65% of the valid votes cast is elected; otherwise a second ballot is held between the two candidates with the most votes.

Vacancies Article 8.9

If a position becomes vacant during a term, the Executive Committee may co-opt an eligible member to fill it until the next General Assembly meeting.

Meetings, quorum and decision-making

Meetings

Annual General Meeting Article 9.4 — An AGM shall be held once in each calendar year, with no more than 15 months between one AGM and the next.

Extraordinary General Meeting Article 9.5 — The Executive Committee shall call an EGM within 28 days of receiving a written request from at least 10% of Eligible Voting Members or 20 Eligible Voting Members, whichever is fewer.

Quorum Article 9.6 — The quorum for an AGM, EGM or General Convention is 20% of Eligible Voting Members or 20 Eligible Voting Members, whichever is fewer, but never fewer than five.

Remote participation Article 9.17 — Meetings may be held in person, remotely or in hybrid form.

Finance, conduct, equality and safeguarding

Finance Article 10

  • All funds and property shall be used only for the Society’s objectives.
  • Where the bank allows, every payment requires approval by two authorised signatories.
  • No person may authorise a payment to themselves or to a connected person.
  • The annual financial statements shall be independently reviewed before they are presented to the AGM.

Counsel of Elders Article 11.8

The Advisory Committee shall, where possible, include respected elders from the community, reflecting the diversity of Nepal’s communities. Elders may be invited to offer guidance on matters of culture and tradition, to support the consensus process, and to help resolve disagreements.

Conduct Article 12

Members and officers shall act with honesty, integrity, respect, fairness and accountability. Disagreements should first be addressed through respectful dialogue, with the aim of restoring good relations rather than assigning blame.

Equality Article 13.1

The Society shall promote equality, dignity and respect in accordance with UK equality law, and respects and celebrates the diversity of Nepalese ethnic, linguistic, cultural and regional identities Article 13.3.

Safeguarding Article 13.5

The Society shall adopt a safeguarding policy and appoint a designated safeguarding lead before running any activity involving children or adults at risk.

Data protection Article 13.6

Personal information shall be collected, used, kept and disclosed only in accordance with data protection law and the Society’s approved procedures.

How we work

Member-led, transparent and accountable

The Society belongs to its members. We make decisions together, choose our leaders democratically, and hold ourselves to high standards of openness and integrity.

Our structure

Power rests with the members

Our structure is simple and democratic. Members make the big decisions; the volunteers they elect carry out the day-to-day work.

General AssemblyAll voting members · the Society’s highest decision-making body
Executive CommitteeElected by members every three years to lead and coordinate the Society’s work
Advisory CommitteeExperienced advisers and respected community elders
Regional LeadsCoordinating activity in local areas across the UK
Working GroupsVolunteer teams for specific projects and events

The General Assembly

Every voting member belongs to the General Assembly and has exactly one vote. Members meet at least once a year at the Annual General Meeting to hear reports, approve the accounts, set priorities and, every three years, elect the Executive Committee. Important decisions — such as changing the Constitution or the membership fee — can only be made by the members themselves.

The Executive Committee

The Executive Committee leads the Society between general meetings. It plans activities, manages finances and represents the Society, always answering to the members. It works through collective leadership: no officer has more voting power than another, and the Chair has no casting vote.

Making decisions

Every voice heard

Inspired by the village assemblies of Nepal, we make decisions by consensus. That does not mean everyone must agree with every detail — it means everyone has the chance to speak, concerns are taken seriously, and we look for a solution the whole group can accept. Voting is a last resort.

  1. Share the proposalProposals are circulated before the meeting so members have time to think and talk with others.
  2. Listen to everyoneEach person is invited to speak in turn, without interruption. We make a special effort to hear elders, young people, women and anyone who has not yet spoken.
  3. Improve the ideaThe group works together to adjust the proposal so that it answers the concerns raised.
  4. Check for agreementMembers say whether they agree, agree with reservations, stand aside, or have a serious objection.
  5. Take time if neededIf a serious objection remains, we pause. A small group — sometimes with a respected elder — looks for a way forward.
  6. Vote as a last resortOnly if agreement still cannot be found do members vote, and even then a strong majority of 65% is needed.

Leadership roles

Who does what

Each elected officer has a clear area of responsibility, so the work of the Society is shared fairly and everyone knows who to turn to.

Chair

Lead Coordinator and Chief Volunteer

Provides overall leadership, chairs Executive Committee meetings, builds consensus and represents the Society externally — always as a facilitator rather than a decision-maker acting alone.

Vice Chair — Membership and Records

Welcomes new members, manages registrations and renewals, keeps the membership register up to date and makes sure members’ information is protected.

Vice Chair — Programmes and Community Engagement

Plans and coordinates the Society’s events and activities, develops the annual programme and reaches out to involve the wider community.

Vice Chair — Fundraising and Resource Development

Develops fundraising plans, identifies funding opportunities, builds relationships with donors and sponsors, and helps secure the Society’s long-term future.

Vice Chair — Partnerships and External Relations

Builds relationships with organisations and institutions in the UK, Nepal and internationally, and supports knowledge exchange and joint initiatives.

Vice Chair — Equality, Diversity and Inclusion

Champions equal opportunities, encourages participation from under-represented groups and advises on making the Society welcoming to all.

General Secretary

Chief Administrative and Governance Coordinator

Manages official correspondence, prepares agendas and papers, keeps minutes and records, and makes sure decisions are followed through.

Treasurer

Finance and Accountability Coordinator

Keeps accurate accounts, oversees banking and payments, reports regularly to the Committee and prepares the annual financial statements for members.

We aim for at least half of our Executive Committee to be women, and we work towards this through fair nominations, encouragement and leadership development.

Elections

Fair and open elections

The Executive Committee is elected by the members for a term of three years, and officers may stand again. Each election is run by an independent Election Committee whose members do not stand as candidates themselves.

Any voting member can stand for election with the nomination of two fellow members. Where only one person stands for a role, members may confirm them by consensus. Where there is a contest, voting takes place by secret ballot, and a candidate needs strong support from the membership to be elected.

If a role becomes vacant during a term, the Committee may appoint an eligible member until the next general meeting, when members elect a replacement.

Members can also call early elections if they feel a change of leadership is needed. Officers who do not fulfil their duties can be removed through a fair process that gives them the chance to respond.

Accountability

Our commitments to members

Financial transparency

Every pound is used for the Society’s aims. Payments need two authorised signatories, no one can approve a payment to themselves, and our annual accounts are independently reviewed before being presented to members.

Regular meetings

Members meet at least once a year at the Annual General Meeting, and can call an extraordinary meeting when needed. Meetings can be held in person, online or both, so everyone can take part.

Wisdom of elders

Our Advisory Committee brings independent expertise and, wherever possible, includes respected elders from Nepal’s diverse communities, who help guide us on culture, tradition and resolving disagreements.

Respectful conduct

We expect honesty, integrity, fairness and respect from everyone. Disagreements are first addressed through dialogue aimed at restoring good relationships, with a fair complaints process when needed.

Equality and safeguarding

We welcome people of every background, celebrate the diversity of Nepal’s communities, and have proper safeguarding in place before any activity involving children or vulnerable adults.

Protecting your data

Members’ personal information is collected and stored securely, used only for Society purposes, and handled in line with UK data protection law.

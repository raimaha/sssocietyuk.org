About us

Our story and purpose

Shram Sanskriti Society UK was founded by members of the Nepalese diaspora who wanted to build a community rooted in service, heritage and mutual respect.

Our story

From shared values to a shared home

The Nepalese community in the United Kingdom is rich and diverse — many ethnicities, languages, faiths and regional traditions living side by side. What unites us is a heritage of working together.

For generations, communities across Nepal have relied on collective labour, mutual aid, respect for elders, harmony with nature and decisions reached by common agreement. These traditions sustained village life through good times and hard ones.

In 2026, a group of community members came together to bring those same values to life in Britain. They formed a Standing Committee, drafted a Constitution with the involvement of members, and on 1 November 2026 the Society was formally established at its First General Convention.

Today, Shram Sanskriti Society UK is a home for anyone who believes in giving back — to their community here in the UK, and to the people and places of Nepal.

Our mission
To unite and engage the Nepalese diaspora in the UK through voluntary service, civic responsibility, cultural preservation and community solidarity.
Our vision
A society founded on dignity, mutual respect, accountability and collective responsibility — in the UK and in Nepal.
Our name
Shram means labour; Sanskriti means culture. Together: a culture that honours work and service.

Our values

Seven values from the heart of Nepal

Our values are drawn from the community traditions shared across Nepal. They shape everything we do — from how we run a meeting to how we choose our projects.

1

Dignity of labour (Shram)

Every kind of work has equal worth — whether it is lifting, cooking, teaching, organising or advising. We honour each person’s contribution and invite everyone to give according to their ability, without hierarchy between manual and intellectual effort.

2

Mutual aid (Parma)

In Nepali villages, neighbours have long worked each other’s fields in turn. We carry that spirit of reciprocity into our community life in Britain: we support one another, and the help we give returns to us all.

3

Decision by agreement

Like the community assemblies of Nepal, we decide together. We take the time to listen, discuss and refine ideas until we reach an outcome the whole community can support.

4

Respect for elders and youth

Our elders carry experience and wisdom that we actively seek out. At the same time, we give young people an equal voice, because the future of our community is theirs to shape.

5

Harmony with nature

We see ourselves as caretakers rather than owners of the natural world. Respect for land, water, forests and wildlife guides the way we live and the projects we support.

6

Equality of communities

Nepal is home to many ethnic, linguistic, cultural and regional communities. Every one of them — and people of every gender — is of equal worth and deserves fair representation within the Society.

7

Leadership as service

Holding office is a responsibility, not a privilege. Our leaders serve the community, remain accountable to the members, and lead by example rather than by authority.

What we aim to achieve

Our aims

Our work is organised around six themes, each reflecting a commitment our members made when they founded the Society.

Service & community

Promoting voluntary service, respect for labour and active participation, and strengthening unity and cooperation among Nepalese communities and the wider British society.

Culture & heritage

Preserving and celebrating the cultural, linguistic, ethnic and historical heritage of Nepal, and passing on languages, oral traditions and customs to younger generations born or raised in the UK.

Civic leadership

Encouraging ethical leadership, transparency, good governance and informed civic engagement, so our members can play an active part in public life.

Inclusion & equality

Making sure women, young people, older people and under-represented groups are heard and involved, and promoting dialogue, non-discrimination and respect for human dignity.

UK–Nepal cooperation

Building bridges between communities in the UK and Nepal through knowledge exchange, skills development and partnerships with organisations that share our goals.

Environment & wellbeing

Supporting community-led initiatives in environmental protection, public awareness, health awareness, heritage preservation and sustainable development.

Our identity

The meaning of our colours

Environment & Nature

Deep Green

We believe the land, water, forests and every living thing deserve our care. We support environmental awareness and community-led projects that protect nature, both here in the United Kingdom and in Nepal.

Science & Technology

Deep Blue

Knowledge grows when it is shared. We encourage education, skills development and the exchange of expertise between our communities in the UK and Nepal, so that learning benefits everyone.

Arts, Culture & Heritage

Deep Yellow

Nepal’s many languages, traditions and art forms are a living inheritance. We celebrate them, preserve them and pass them on, especially to young people growing up in Britain.

What we do

Turning values into action

We bring our aims to life through a wide range of activities, planned and delivered by volunteers. These include cultural programmes and celebrations, community events, seminars and workshops, educational and environmental projects, and voluntary-service initiatives in the UK and Nepal.

We work in partnership with organisations and institutions that share our goals, and we publish information and learning resources for our members and the wider public. Our activities are funded through membership subscriptions, donations, grants, sponsorship and fundraising events — always managed transparently and used only for the Society’s aims.

Independent and non-profit

SSS UK is an independent, voluntary and non-profit association. We govern ourselves, manage our own finances and make our own decisions. No external organisation controls the Society.

We are inspired by the Shram Sanskriti movement in Nepal and its values of community service, and we welcome lawful partnerships with like-minded organisations. We engage in civic education and community dialogue, but we do not operate as a political party.
